Homogenization in AI

Social Impacts, Pluralism, and Agents

AI systems increasingly act autonomously, interacting with users and with one another. Together they form interconnected ecosystems that influence whose perspectives are represented, which decisions are made, and who bears the consequences.

This bridge focuses on homogenization — the tendency of distinct AI systems to converge in their behaviors, outputs and values — its social impacts, and how pluralism can help preserve diverse perspectives, values and interests in agentic ecosystems.

Challenges

The bridge is organized around three related challenges: understanding the social impacts of homogenization, promoting pluralism, and studying how agentic systems affect both.

I

Social impacts of homogenization

  • What social harms emerge when AI systems become increasingly homogenized? Which communities are most vulnerable?
  • How can we operationalize metrics for homogenization of creative expression, language preferences and downstream utility — beyond discrete predictive settings?
II

Promoting pluralism in AI

  • How can diversity be preserved and promoted in the face of homogenized AI systems, while keeping the benefits that scale may provide?
  • To what extent do shared pre-training data, architectures and overlapping benchmarks drive downstream homogenization — and how can we prioritize pluralism under shared infrastructure?
III

Homogenization in agentic systems

  • How can agent interactions amplify shared failures, create conformity towards the majority, and polarize otherwise moderate positions?
  • How can pluralism be encouraged and preserved under the pressures of conformity and sycophancy in agent interactions?

Call for Papers

Research Track

6–9 pages

Novel contributions on any of the three challenges, such as metrics for homogenization, its social impacts, methods for preserving pluralism, or conformity in interacting agents. Selected papers are presented as 5-minute contributed talks; all others as posters.

Proposal Track

up to 3 pages

Proposals on operationalizing research on homogenization, including interdisciplinary proposals that examine current approaches. Intended to support researchers without the compute to run experiments. Selected proposals are presented as 3-minute talks.

Community Perspectives

1 page

One-page perspectives on an open problem or a direction the community should prioritize around homogenization and pluralism. A selection is presented as short talks in the Community Perspectives session, a format first run at MURE@AAAI 2026.
